1. LoRA and progressive backbone unfreezing (Sections 4.3 and 4.4)

We agree that the original descriptions did not clearly distinguish two
completed training phases. The preliminary hyperparameter sweeps used standard
LoRA with the backbone frozen throughout training. The final runs plotted in the
main test-set comparison used LoRA with progressive backbone unfreezing.
Sections 4.3 and 4.4 now describe both procedures explicitly. The abstract,
results, discussion, and conclusion distinguish the phases and avoid attributing
the hybrid's outcomes to low-rank updates alone.

The preliminary AdaFace sweeps used 40 epochs, batch size 8, QKV-only adapters,
no gradual unfreezing, and no backbone-bias training. The selected Swin-Base and
DeiT3-Base configurations achieved EVAL rank-1 rates of 0.5814 and 0.5891,
respectively. These are hyperparameter-selection metrics, distinct from the
test-set rates plotted for the later hybrid models; they are now reported with
their split and procedure explicitly identified.

Section 4.4 now gives the complete schedule, using zero-based epoch indices.
During epochs 0-4, only the adapters and AdaFace classification head were
trainable. Swin's final stage was unfrozen at epoch 5 and its preceding stage at
epoch 10. DeiT-3's final eight of twelve blocks were unfrozen one at a time,
deepest first, at epochs 5, 10, ..., 40. All original weights within each selected
module became trainable, including its attention projections, FFN, and
normalization weights. The remaining backbone components stayed frozen.

A new trainability table reports the percentage of original backbone parameters
trainable at successive stages. The final percentages were 95.65%, 97.55%, and
97.55% for Swin-T/S/B, and 65.71%, 65.95%, and 66.18% for DeiT3-S/M/B. Adapters
and the classification head are excluded from these percentages and remained
trainable throughout. Counts were reconstructed from the implemented callbacks
and saved run settings, rather than presented as newly measured training results.
We also report the actual adapter ranks in the final hybrid runs: 24 for
Swin-T/S, 8 for Swin-B, and 4 for DeiT-3. The text distinguishes the complete
50-epoch schedule from the earlier checkpoint selected by EVAL rank-1 for test
evaluation. For example, the selected DeiT3-Base hybrid checkpoint is from epoch
8, when one block (8.27% of original backbone parameters) had been unfrozen.

The general account of standard LoRA in Section 3.3 remains distinct from this
experimental hybrid. Its discussion now makes clear that frozen-backbone
optimizer-state and adapter-only update-payload savings cannot be assumed for
our progressively unfrozen runs. Section 4.5 also clarifies that Direct KD used
progressive unfreezing without adapters, whereas LoRA KD added adapters to that
schedule. Auditing the comparison runs also corrected the baseline description:
the paired baselines used 50 epochs, batch size 8, and progressive unfreezing
without adapters, not 100-epoch full fine-tuning. The architecture-survey methods
now reflect the saved per-family configurations, including batch size 16 and an
ArcFace head for ResNet. These corrections change the training description, not
the recorded accuracy results.

2. Multi-head attention terminology (Section 2.1 and Figure 1)

We now consistently call the encoder mechanism multi-head self-attention (MHSA).
Section 2.1 explains that multi-head attention is the broader mechanism:
self-attention derives queries, keys, and values from the same sequence, whereas
cross-attention derives queries from a different sequence than keys and values.
The caption of Figure 1 explicitly explains that the original source diagram's
label "Multi-Head Attention" denotes MHSA in this encoder. Figure 2's caption
uses the same spelling and abbreviation as the text.

3. Definition and example of patch embeddings (Section 2.1)

We added an explicit definition: a patch embedding is the fixed-length vector
obtained by a shared learned linear projection of a flattened image patch. A
small example divides an 8x8 RGB image into four 4x4 patches, orders them by rows,
and projects each patch's 48 pixel values to a 12-dimensional token. We connect
this example to the evaluated DeiT-3 inputs: a 96x96 image with 16x16 patches
produces a 6x6 grid, or 36 patch tokens before adding the classification token.

4. Swin's accuracy and computational efficiency (Section 2.4)

We expanded the explanation of local-window MHSA, alternating regular and
shifted partitions, and hierarchical patch merging. Shifted partitions allow
information to cross window boundaries, while patch merging builds broader
context at coarser spatial scales. The text distinguishes these mechanisms for
preserving useful representations from a guarantee of unchanged task accuracy.
It also states that the attention-interaction term changes from O(N^2 D) for
global attention to O(N M^2 D) for fixed M-by-M windows.

5. Space before the sparsity citation (Section 3.5)

We inserted a nonbreaking space before the citation following "sparsity".

6. Final TensorRT conversion outcome (Abstract and Section 5.7)

The abstract now distinguishes out-of-the-box conversion failures from the final
deployment result. It states that per-family module-level rewrites enabled
native TensorRT inference for all 16 architectures. This agrees with the
successful conversion procedure and unified native-TensorRT benchmarks already
reported in Section 5.7.
